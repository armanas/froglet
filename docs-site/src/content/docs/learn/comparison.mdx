---
title: How Froglet compares
description: Where Froglet overlaps with payment and commerce protocols, and where their responsibilities differ.
---

Froglet helps people and agents offer a service, agree the terms of a request,
run it, and keep signed records. Payment is one part of that process; a free
service uses the same offer, quote, deal, and receipt flow.

Payment and commerce protocols overlap with parts of this work. Their open
specifications and the requirements of a particular hosted product are
separate things.

## Different responsibilities

| Project | Main focus | Read the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Froglet** | Service offers, per-request commitments, execution limits, and signed outcome records. | [Deal flow](/learn/deal-flow/) and [kernel](/spec/kernel/) |
| **A2A** | Agent discovery and task communication, including capability and extension negotiation. The agent's implementation supplies execution. | [A2A specification](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/) |
| **x402** | Payment over HTTP: a server requests payment and a client retries with payment information. | [x402 documentation](https://x402.org/) |
| **L402** | Paid access to digital resources using a token bound to a Lightning invoice and its payment preimage. | [Lightning Labs documentation](https://docs.lightning.engineering/the-lightning-network/l402) |
| **AP2** | Evidence of authorization for agent payments, using verifiable credentials and mandates. | [AP2 specification](https://ap2-protocol.org/) |
| **ACP** | Agent commerce, including checkout and merchant fulfillment, with support for different payment processors. | [ACP specification](https://www.agenticcommerce.dev/) |

AP2 is broader than moving money, and ACP is not inherently limited to a
Stripe account or a ChatGPT seller application. A particular integration may
impose onboarding requirements beyond the protocol itself.

## What Froglet's records establish

A verifier can check the signatures and hash bindings across the descriptor,
offer, quote, deal, and receipt, plus the invoice bundle when the chosen rail
requires one. These records identify the signing keys, the agreed terms, and
the provider's reported outcome. A result hash binds a receipt to particular
result bytes.

This is **evidence of commitments and reported outcomes**, not a cryptographic
proof that an arbitrary computation was performed correctly. The same care
applies to settlement: what can be verified depends on the payment method.
See [Trust & Economics](/learn/economics/) and [Settlement](/learn/settlement/).

## What to evaluate before choosing

- **The task:** sharing selected data, selling API access, authorizing a purchase,
  and completing a shopping checkout have different requirements.
- **Deployment:** assess the actual implementation, hosting, credentials, and
  payment infrastructure you will operate, as well as the specification.
- **Recourse:** signatures make claims attributable; they do not automatically
  provide refunds, chargebacks, or enforce a legal agreement.
- **Maturity:** Froglet is a public beta. Its supported first-use journey is free
  sharing. Lightning has recorded regtest evidence, Stripe is test-mode, and
  x402 is a daemon-level prototype outside the publish path. See the
  [current payment guide](/learn/payment-rails/).

## Possible combinations

MCP connects an assistant to Froglet's native tool. The optional A2A adapter
connects configured requester and provider Nodes using A2A 1.0 HTTP+JSON and a
required Froglet extension. The Node supplies the bounded Wasm executor and
signed deal lifecycle behind those interfaces. See the
[working profile and local demo](/learn/agent-interoperability/).

A2A plus a payment adapter and a suitable executor could support a similar
application. Froglet is a particular implementation of the execution controls
and transaction lifecycle; the claim is not that those capabilities are
fundamentally unavailable in another stack.

An authorization system could authorize a Froglet request, and a payment
adapter could settle it. These are architectural possibilities, not claims
that AP2, ACP, or L402 interoperability has been implemented and tested.
Froglet's experimental x402 adapter has its own documented limits.

This comparison describes responsibilities, not adoption rankings. The A2A
specification and current Froglet integration were checked on 1 October 2026;
the other primary sources were checked on 27 September 2026. Implementation
status can change.
